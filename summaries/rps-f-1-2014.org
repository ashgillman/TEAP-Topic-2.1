* Authority and jurisdiction
- Australian radiation-protection fundamentals issued by ARPANSA.
- A high-level, non-mandatory statement of objectives and principles; it does not itself create Queensland legal duties.
- Informs national codes and standards and closely reflects the IAEA fundamental safety objective and principles.

* Key takeaways :AI:
- The fundamental safety objective is protection of people and the environment from harmful effects of ionising radiation without unduly limiting beneficial uses.
- Justification, optimisation and limitation form the conceptual basis of the THHS RSPP, but their application differs for planned occupational/public exposure and patient medical exposure.
- Primary responsibility rests with the person or organisation responsible for facilities and activities that give rise to radiation risks.
- Effective protection also needs government and regulatory infrastructure, leadership, management for safety, prevention, preparedness and learning.
- Safety and source security are interdependent and should be managed coherently without disclosing sensitive controls.

* Important definitions, roles, limits and records
- /Justification/ :: A facility or activity should produce sufficient benefit to outweigh the radiation detriment it causes.
- /Optimisation/ :: Protection should provide the highest level reasonably achievable, taking economic and societal factors into account.
- /Limitation/ :: Measures should ensure no individual bears an unacceptable radiation risk; detailed numerical limits are set in lower-tier instruments.
- /Responsible person or organisation/ :: Retains primary responsibility even where functions are delegated.
- The Fundamentals do not prescribe local record-retention periods or clinical tolerances.

* Practical relationship to the THHS RSPP
- Supplies the conceptual basis for the RSPP's stated outcomes, safety culture, defence in depth, ALARA approach and allocation of responsibility.
- Supports reading the RSPP as a management system spanning normal operation, prevention, emergency readiness and continuous improvement.
- It should be cited as a principle source, not as the immediate legal authority for a specific THHS action.
