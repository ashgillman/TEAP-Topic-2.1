1. Source documents

  • Official IAEA PDF — checked 29 September 2026.

2. Authority and jurisdiction

  • International implementing guide for security of radioactive material in use and storage and associated facilities. It is guidance, not Queensland law.
  • Whether a particular HDR source is subject to Queensland security-enhanced-source requirements depends on its legal categorisation and approved arrangements.

3. Key takeaways

  • Security measures are graded to the source and threat context and address unauthorised removal or sabotage.
  • Management includes access control, source accountancy, inventory, information protection, training and response.
  • Public study material should not expose sensitive site-specific security arrangements.

4. Summary

4.1. Sections 1–3 — Scope and State security regime

  • Explains objectives, responsibilities, categorisation and the interface between radiation safety and security.

4.2. Section 4 — Security of radioactive material in use and storage

  • Describes graded security functions and management, including access control and accountancy. This summary intentionally omits operational details.

5. Practical relationship to the THHS RSPP

  • Provides international context for the RSPP's HDR source security and inventory concepts. Queensland Regulation Part 5 and the prescribed Australian security code give the applicable local regulatory path.
