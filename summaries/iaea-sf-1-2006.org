* Authority and jurisdiction
- IAEA Safety Fundamentals publication stating the international fundamental safety objective and ten principles.
- International consensus-level fundamentals, not legislation and not direct regulation of a Queensland hospital.
- Provides the conceptual basis for IAEA requirements and guidance and strongly influences corresponding Australian fundamentals.

* Key takeaways :AI:
- The fundamental safety objective is to protect people and the environment from harmful effects of ionising radiation.
- Primary responsibility for safety rests with the person or organisation responsible for facilities and activities that create radiation risks.
- Government must establish an effective legal and regulatory framework; international publications do not displace the jurisdictional regulator.
- Justification and optimisation are central, while risks to individuals must be limited through the applicable protection system.
- Leadership, accident prevention, emergency preparedness and protective action are necessary parts of safety, not additions after routine controls fail.

* Summary :AI:
** 1 — Introduction
- Explains scope, status, application and the relationship between Safety Fundamentals, Safety Requirements and Safety Guides.

** 2 — Safety objective
- States the objective of protecting people and the environment while allowing beneficial activities involving radiation to proceed under proper control.

** 3 — Fundamental safety principles
1. Responsibility for safety.
2. Role of government.
3. Leadership and management for safety.
4. Justification of facilities and activities.
5. Optimisation of protection.
6. Limitation of risks to individuals.
7. Protection of present and future generations.
8. Prevention of accidents.
9. Emergency preparedness and response.
10. Protective actions to reduce existing or unregulated radiation risks.

* Important definitions, roles, limits and records
- /Safety/ :: Protection of people and the environment against radiation risks and safety of facilities and activities that give rise to those risks.
- /Primary responsibility/ :: Remains with the responsible person or organisation and cannot be transferred to the regulator.
- SF-1 gives principles rather than numerical clinical tolerances, detailed dose limits or record-retention rules.
- Records are implied as part of effective management, demonstration and learning; detailed requirements occur in lower-tier standards and law.

* Practical relationship to the THHS RSPP
- Provides the top-level international rationale for the RSPP's responsibility, governance, justification, optimisation, defence in depth, emergency readiness and learning systems.
- The operational chain runs through IAEA requirements/guidance, Australian frameworks, Queensland law and the approved local plan; SF-1 does not directly require a THHS procedure.
