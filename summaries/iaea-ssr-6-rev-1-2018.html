1. Source documents

  • Official IAEA PDF — 2018 edition; checked 29 September 2026.
  • Current IAEA SSR-6 (Rev. 2), 2025 edition.

2. Authority and jurisdiction

  • IAEA international transport safety requirements. Rev. 1 was superseded internationally by Rev. 2 (2025).
  • RPS C-2 (2019) Schedule A incorporates the 2018 edition for the Australian code. The later IAEA edition does not automatically amend the Queensland-prescribed code.

3. Key takeaways

  • Transport safety uses graded material and package classification, package performance, dose-rate and contamination controls, marking, labelling, documents and carriage requirements.
  • Consignor, carrier and consignee duties extend across preparation, transport and receipt.
  • Security of a source during transport is a related but distinct subject; see IAEA NSS No. 46-T and Queensland transport security requirements where applicable.

4. Summary

4.1. Sections I–III — Scope, definitions and general provisions

  • Establishes the scope, radiation-protection programme, training and management provisions.

4.2. Section IV — Activity limits and material classification

  • Classifies radioactive material and defines activity limits used in package selection.

4.3. Section V — Transport requirements and controls

  • Covers package categories, dose-rate and contamination controls, marking and labelling, transport documents and responsibilities through carriage.

4.4. Sections VI–VIII — Package requirements, tests and approvals

  • Establishes graded package performance, testing and administrative approvals.

5. Practical relationship to the THHS RSPP

  • Provides the international transport-safety basis incorporated into RPS C-2 (2019). Check the current Australian code and Queensland legal mechanism for binding obligations.
  • The local HDR source transport procedure must also address any applicable source-security controls.
